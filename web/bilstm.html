The core research question
Does temporal frame-level modelling outperform deep statistical compression on short, controlled studio recordings?
The answer is nuanced. On in-domain RAVDESS data with augmentation, both CNN-B (93.98%) and BiLSTM (93.52%) achieve comparable performance. However, the BiLSTM (augmented) achieves 26.1% on CREMA-D — 6.2 percentage points better than CNN-B's 19.9%. Temporal sequence modelling encodes slightly more speaker-independent acoustic information than static statistical compression, making it a better generaliser despite equivalent in-domain performance.
Architectural distinction

Temporal vs statistical compression

CNN-A / CNN-B input
(N, 524, 1)
Statistical compression: each feature type is summarised as mean and standard deviation across all frames. Temporal order is completely discarded. The model sees a snapshot of the utterance, not its evolution over time.
BiLSTM input
(N, 130, 120)
Temporal preservation: 130 analysis frames (~23 ms each), each containing 40 MFCCs + 40 Deltas + 40 Delta-Deltas = 120 features. The model can learn how spectral patterns change over the course of the utterance — including prosodic dynamics like rising pitch in surprise or falling energy in sadness.

RAVDESS is an offline corpus — full utterances are available at inference time, making bidirectional processing valid. The forward LSTM reads frames left-to-right, the backward LSTM reads right-to-left; both are concatenated at each timestep, doubling the hidden state.

✦ Cross-dataset silver lining
The strongest generaliser
While CNN-B achieves higher in-domain accuracy (93.98% vs 93.52%), the BiLSTM (augmented) is the only model that reaches 26.1% on CREMA-D — versus 19.9% for CNN-B (augmented). The gap between RAVDESS accuracy and CREMA-D accuracy (−67.4pp) is smaller for BiLSTM than for any other model tested. Temporal sequence modelling encodes slightly more speaker-independent acoustic information than static statistical compression, because it learns how spectral patterns transition over time rather than just what their average values are.
