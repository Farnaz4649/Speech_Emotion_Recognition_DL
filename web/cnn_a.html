⚠ Bottleneck — the Flatten problem

After Block 3's MaxPool, the output shape is (62, 256). Flattening produces a 15,872-dimensional vector. The subsequent Dense(128) layer alone adds 15,872 × 128 = 2,031,744 weights — 91% of the total parameter count. This massive head is the primary source of overfitting on a dataset of 6,048 training samples.

Why augmentation barely helps CNN-A

With 2.24M parameters and only 6,048 training samples, CNN-A is already in an extreme overfitting regime. The massive Dense head memorises training recordings rather than learning generalisable acoustic patterns. Adding more data variants (+0.35pp) cannot overcome a fundamentally overparameterised architecture — the bottleneck must be redesigned, not just fed more data. CNN-B addresses this directly.
