83% reduction in parameters compared to CNN-A (2,238,344 → 365,128), yet CNN-B achieves 4.22 percentage points higher augmented accuracy (93.98% vs 89.76%).

This result directly demonstrates that architectural regularisation — via Batch Normalisation and Global Average Pooling — is more effective than simply adding data when working with small corpora.
Three critical upgrades over CNN-A

What changed

01
Extra Convolutional Block
A fourth block doubles the filter depth to 512, allowing the network to learn more abstract, emotion-discriminative spectral patterns. Filter progression: 64 → 128 → 256 → 512.
02
Batch Normalisation
Applied after every Conv1D layer. Stabilises gradient flow and reduces sensitivity to weight initialisation — particularly important on a dataset of 6,048 training samples where unstable training can waste many epochs.
03
Global Average Pooling
Replaces the Flatten layer of CNN-A. Instead of producing a 15,872-dimensional vector, GAP averages each filter map across all 61 spatial positions, yielding a compact 512-dimensional vector. This eliminates 91% of CNN-A's parameters.

Why slower convergence is better

CNN-A converges in 29 epochs — a warning sign on a small dataset. Fast convergence to a sharp local minimum generalises poorly. CNN-B, aided by Batch Normalisation and the lower learning rate (0.0005 vs 0.001), takes 62–82 epochs to converge, finding a flatter, more generalisable solution. The augmented model's +3.70pp gain confirms that the GAP head is not saturated and continues to benefit from more diverse training examples.
