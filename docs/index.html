Dataset · EDA

RAVDESS — dataset overview

2,880 recordings from 24 professional actors (12 male, 12 female), covering 8 emotion classes at two intensity levels. The dataset is deliberately balanced — neutral is the only class with 192 samples rather than 384, because it has no "strong" intensity variant. Balance means no class-weighting is needed during training.

All classes contain 192 male + 192 female recordings (384 total) except Neutral, which has 96 + 96 = 192, as there is no strong-intensity neutral variant in RAVDESS.

Cross-dataset evaluation

The generalisation gap

All models trained on RAVDESS were evaluated on 900 CREMA-D clips — a completely different corpus with different actors, phonetics, and recording conditions.

Key finding
The Benchmark Illusion

Accuracy above 93% on a controlled, acted dataset is not a valid proxy for real-world deployability. All models collapsed by more than 67 percentage points when evaluated on unseen speakers and environments.

✦ Silver lining — BiLSTM (augmented) proved the strongest generaliser at 26.1%, versus 19.9% for CNN-B (augmented). Temporal sequence modelling encodes slightly more speaker-independent information than static statistical compression.

Conclusions & Reflections

Three key takeaways

01
Depth + Regularisation Dominates Shallow Baselines
CNN-B vastly outperformed CNN-A despite having 83% fewer parameters. Global Average Pooling effectively eliminates the overfitting bottleneck inherent to Flatten-to-Dense networks on small datasets.
02
Temporal Models Have Higher Sample Complexity
BiLSTM requires significantly more data to learn temporal transitions. Given sufficient augmentation, it matches the performance of deep CNNs and displays superior cross-domain robustness (+6.2 pp over CNN-B on CREMA-D).
03
High In-Domain Accuracy Alone Is Misleading
The severe collapse on CREMA-D proves that >93% accuracy on a controlled corpus is not a valid proxy for real-world deployability. Genuine progress requires domain-agnostic benchmarks and multi-corpus training.

Limitations & future work

Core limitations

  • ! Domain gap. RAVDESS relies on acted, controlled studio speech, which inflates accuracy relative to naturalistic conversation.
  • ! Narrow context. Models overfit to the phonetic and prosodic constraints of the two fixed sentences used in RAVDESS.
  • ! Simple augmentation. Gaussian noise and time-shifting are insufficient to bridge multi-domain acoustic environments.

Actionable future directions

  • → Pretrained representations. Replace handcrafted MFCCs with self-supervised extractors like wav2vec 2.0 or HuBERT for domain-agnostic features.
  • → Multi-corpus training. Train simultaneously on RAVDESS, CREMA-D, and IEMOCAP to force generalised emotional boundaries.
  • → Architectural enhancements. Add attention mechanisms to the BiLSTM and apply richer augmentations such as SpecAugment and pitch shifting.
