Design rationale

Key architectural decisions

01
Batch Normalisation After Every Conv
Applied after each of the four Conv1D layers. Normalises activations within each mini-batch, stabilising gradient flow and reducing sensitivity to weight initialisation — particularly valuable on a dataset of 6,048 training samples.
02
Decreasing Filter Depth
Filters progress 256→128→64→64, wider at the input and narrower deeper in the network. The first block extracts a broad set of low-level spectral features across 256 channels; subsequent blocks compress and refine these into increasingly abstract representations.
03
Four Pooling Stages
Each block uses MaxPool(size=5, stride=2, padding=same), compressing the 524-length sequence to 262→131→66→33 across four stages. This reduces the Flatten output to just 2,112 values, keeping the Dense head compact at 135K weights versus CNN-A's 2.03M.

15,872 → Dense(128)
62 × 256 values flattened. The Dense(128) head alone requires 15,872 × 128 = 2.03M weights — 91% of CNN-A's total parameter count. Primary source of overfitting.
CNN-B — compact flatten
2,112 → Dense(64)
33 × 64 values flattened after four pooling stages. The Dense(64) head requires only 2,112 × 64 = 135K weights. The pooling layers do the spatial compression so the head does not have to.

Why slower convergence is better

CNN-A converges in 29 epochs — a warning sign on a small dataset. Fast convergence to a sharp local minimum generalises poorly. CNN-B, aided by Batch Normalisation at every convolutional layer, takes 62–82 epochs to reach its best validation loss, finding a flatter and more generalisable solution. The augmented model's +3.70pp gain confirms that the architecture benefits from diverse training examples rather than saturating early.
